## In-text annotations

 <mark class="hltr-yellow">Justice ALITO, with whom Justice THOMAS and Justice GORSUCH join, concurring in the judgment.</mark>  ([Page 7](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/HR9I6VBQ?page=7&annotation=VMCAPT88)) 





 <mark class="hltr-yellow">This Court has long recognized that the Act does not "invest those who go on strike with an immunity from discharge for acts of trespass or violence against the employer's property." NLRB v. Fansteel Metallurgical Corp., 306 U.S. 240, 255, 59 S.Ct. 490, 83 L.Ed. 627 (1939). To justify "despoiling [an employer's] property" or "the seizure and conversion of its goods," we have reasoned, "would be to put a premium on resort to force instead of legal remedies." Id., at 253, 59 S.Ct. 490. It follows that Garmon preemption does not prevent States from imposing liability on employees who intentionally destroy their employer's property. See, e.g., Machinists v. Wisconsin Employment Relations Comm'n, 427 U.S. 132, 136, 96 S.Ct. 2548, 49 L.Ed.2d 396 (1976) ("Policing ... destruction of property has been held most clearly a matter for the States"); Construction Workers v. Laburnum Constr. Corp., 347 U.S. 656, 669, 74 S.Ct. 833, 98 L.Ed. 1025 (1954) (The NLRA does not allow employees to "destroy property without liability for the damage done"); Electrical Workers v. Wisconsin Employment Relations Bd., 315 U.S. 740, 748, 62 S.Ct. 820, 86 L.Ed. 1154 (1942) (The NLRA "was not designed to preclude a State" from regulating threats of property damage); see also Linn v. Plant Guard Workers, 383 U.S. 53, 61-62, 86 S.Ct. 657, 15 L.Ed.2d 582 (1966) ("`[T]here is no ground for concluding that existing criminal penalties or liabilities for tortious conduct have been eliminated'" by the NLRA); Bill Johnson's Restaurants, Inc. v. NLRB, 461 U.S. 731, 741-742, 103 S.Ct. 2161, 76 L.Ed.2d 277 (1983) ("It has ... repeatedly been held that an employer has the right to seek local judicial protection from tortious conduct during a labor dispute").</mark>  ([Page 7](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/HR9I6VBQ?page=7&annotation=VNNZ4YAK))
