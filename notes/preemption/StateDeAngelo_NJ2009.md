## In-text annotations

 <mark class="hltr-yellow">The question presented is whether a municipal sign ordinance that prohibited all but a few exempted signs and that expressly prohibited "portable signs[,] balloon signs or other inflated signs (excepting grand opening signs)," can be applied to prohibit a union from displaying as part of its labor protest a large inflatable rat, a symbol of labor unrest. T</mark>  ([Page 1](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/4MPUV2S7?page=1&annotation=highlight-p1x72y201)) 





 <mark class="hltr-yellow">Before us, the union official claims in part that the ordinance violates the free speech protections guaranteed by the Federal and New Jersey Constitutions, U.S. Const. amend. I; N.J. Const. art. I, § 6. We agree, and reverse.</mark>  ([Page 1](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/4MPUV2S7?page=1&annotation=highlight-p1x72y138)) 





 <mark class="hltr-yellow">We conclude that the Township sign ordinance violates the First Amendment right to free speech and is overbroad; it therefore cannot stand. As a result, defendant's conviction predicated on an asserted violation of the sign ordinance must be set aside. The judgment of the Appellate Division is reversed, and the case is remanded to the Law Division for the entry of an order dismissing the summons issued to defendant.</mark>  ([Page 8](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/4MPUV2S7?page=8&annotation=highlight-p8x72y662))
